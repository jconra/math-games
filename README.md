This is a project for random math games that can be played in the browser. The goal is to help people who want to practice some math and have fun.

## Credits

### Mathwood Valley

- Farm animals (the cow, horse, pig, sheep and the pug you can buy as a dog): [LowPoly Animated Farm Animal Pack](https://opengameart.org/node/85047) by Quaternius, CC0. Exported from the pack's .blend files; the pig, sheep and pug walks were refitted to their shorter legs, and the pug's sit, lie and trot were made for the game.
- The cabin's furniture stand-ins (models/furniture/, see its CREDITS.md): the bed ([Vintage Day Bed](https://polyhaven.com/a/vintage_day_bed)), the table ([Wooden Table 02](https://polyhaven.com/a/wooden_table_02)), the chairs ([Painted Wooden Chair 01](https://polyhaven.com/a/painted_wooden_chair_01)), the painting ([Fancy Picture Frame 01](https://polyhaven.com/a/fancy_picture_frame_01)) and the porch rockers ([Rocking Chair 01](https://polyhaven.com/a/Rockingchair_01)), all from Poly Haven, CC0. The rug, lanterns, dog beds, rifle rack, stove and the keepsakes are made in code.
- The stage music (harmonica and piano) is made in the browser as it plays: no recordings. The two instruments are patches for the [Sound Lab](https://github.com/jconra/sound-lab) engine, and the tune is made up as it goes.
- The band's old fiddle tunes (Soldier's Joy, Old Joe Clark, Arkansas Traveler, Sally Goodin, Cripple Creek, Devil's Dream, Fisher's Hornpipe) are traditional (public domain). The notes come from written versions of the tunes (thesession.org, natunelist.net, colinhume.com, the Combined Tunebook, the Traditional Tune Archive, O'Neill's Music of Ireland), arranged for the band in the game; no recordings. Oh! Susanna (1848) and Camptown Races (1850) are Stephen Foster's songs (public domain), their melodies written down for the band from the songs as they're sung, the chords the band's own. The William Tell gallop (from Rossini's William Tell Overture, 1829) and In the Hall of the Mountain King (Grieg, 1875) are public domain; their themes are written down for the band and arranged in the game's own way. The sea shanties Drunken Sailor, Wellerman, Blow the Man Down and Haul Away Joe, and the river song Shenandoah, are traditional (public domain), written down for the band from the songs as they're sung, arranged in the game's own way.
- The band's fiddle is a real violin, sampled: the solo violin from [VSCO 2 Community Edition](https://github.com/sgossner/VSCO-2-CE) by Versilian Studios, CC0 (mathwood-valley/sounds/fiddle/, see sounds/CREDITS.md).
- Kept for later, not played yet: an upright piano (Ivy Audio's Upright Piano, VSCO 2 Community Edition, CC0) and a banjo (the Fluid R3 soundfont's by Frank Wen, via gleitz/midi-js-soundfonts, CC BY 3.0); see mathwood-valley/sounds/CREDITS.md.
- Cowboy voice clips (the player's "Hyah!", "Whoa", "Yeehaw!", "Howdy" and the rest, and the townsmen's and bandits' "Much obliged", "You got me" and so on, mathwood-valley/sounds/voice/): recorded by the game's maker.
- Read-aloud (the Counting, Adding and Taking away cards read out loud, with "That's right!", "Not that one." and "Count 'em again.", mathwood-valley/sounds/read/): the numbers and words recorded by the game's maker, cut into short clips that the game joins into each card's line.
- The town's animal and bird sounds (hens, a rooster, cows, sheep, horses, the dogs, a meadowlark, crows, ravens, a hawk) are short recordings, every one CC0 or public domain, from [BigSoundBank](https://bigsoundbank.com) (Joseph SARDIN and contributors), the US National Park Service's Yellowstone sound library, Wikimedia Commons and OpenGameArt.org: each clip with its source page, author and licence in mathwood-valley/sounds/CREDITS.md. The hammering and sawing out back, the hooves, the creek and the pigs' grunts are made in the browser, no recordings.
- The action music for "Protect the wagon" (the galloping drums, plucked bass, twangy guitar, whistled tune, whip cracks and the big drum on a catch) is made in the browser as it plays too: seven Sound Lab patches, a tune made up as it goes, no recordings.
- The hats at the general store: the cowboy and white hats are the farmer's hat from Quaternius' characters (CC0); the straw hat, bowler, ranger hat, cattleman and sombrero are shaped in code. The horses you ride and buy (models/animals/steed.glb) are the game maker's own model, made with Meshy from his photo of a bay, bound in Blender to the Quaternius horse's skeleton so they keep its animations; their coats, markings, mane and tail are drawn in code. The donkey and the wagon teams' farm-pack horse are still the Quaternius ones.
- The town's buildings (the stable, the sheriff's office and jail, the general store, the saloon, the blacksmith and the houses) are built in code from parts, with their wood, stone, paint and signs drawn in code; the player's cabin is one of the code-built cabin designs too.
- The mill on the hill (models/town/mill.glb): the game maker's own model, made with Meshy; sized to life and reduced for the game. The windmill tower, the bridge and the dock are made in code.
- The camps out in the valley (models/camps/, see its CREDITS.md): the cow camp, the tipi camp and the surveyors' wall tent are the game maker's own models, made with Tripo; each was joined into one shape, sized to life and its picture shrunk for the game.
- The wagons on the road (models/wagons/, see its CREDITS.md): the covered wagon, the chuck wagon, the farm wagon and the freight wagon are the game maker's own models, made with Tripo; each was joined into one shape, given one shared wheel (rebuilt from the farm wagon's back wheel) that turns, sized to life and its picture shrunk for the game. The teams are the farm-pack horse (above); the harness is made in code. The rumble, creaks and hooves are made in the browser (three Sound Lab patches), no recordings.
- The wild deer and the stag: the deer and stag from the [Ultimate Animated Animal Pack](https://poly.pizza/bundle/Animated-Animal-Pack-ILAPXeUYiS) by Quaternius, CC0. Old Hob's box camera on its tripod (with its flash and puff of smoke) is made in code; the shutter's click and the flash powder's whoomp are made in the browser, no recordings.
- The bottle range (bottles and tin cans only, nothing alive): the six-shooter in the rider's hand, the shelf, the hay bales, the fences, the bottles and the tin cans are made in code; the gunshot, the clank of a can, the bursting glass, the glancing shot and the hammer's click are made in the browser (six Sound Lab patches), no recordings.
- The gang's cache (Loot the gang): the canvas tent, the sacks of loot and the iron-banded strongbox are made in code; the crates are the camps' own crate. The townsman's "Is that my stuff?" and the thank-yous are the game maker's own recorded voice.
- The campfires' flames: [VolumetricFire](https://github.com/yomotsu/VolumetricFire) by Akihiro Oyamada (yomotsu), MIT licence, ported from the [WebGL fire demo](http://webgl-fire.appspot.com/html/fire.html); src/fire/VolumetricFire.js with its two pictures (firetex.png, nzw.png), by way of the Shipwrecked game's campfire and the jconra.com fire lab. The crackle is made in the browser (two Sound Lab patches), no recordings.
